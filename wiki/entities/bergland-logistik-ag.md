# Bergland Logistik AG

Customer of [[alpstein-robotics-ag]], based in Buchs. (Source: [[2026-03-12-executive-board-minutes]])

## In the sources

- Two pilot robots of AlpPick 2.0 have been running at Bergland in Buchs since February 2026 ([[alppick-2-0-launch]], [[alppick]]). (Source: [[2026-03-12-executive-board-minutes]])

## Downtime Hall 3 (May/June 2026)

- Contact: [[thomas-rueegg]], Head of Logistics. (Source: [[2026-06-18-email-thread-bergland]])
- Third downtime incident in four weeks; on 15 June 2026 four AlpPick robots stood still for almost two hours. (Source: [[2026-06-18-email-thread-bergland]])
- Downtime costs Bergland about CHF 4,000 per hour (statement by Rüegg, 16 June 2026). (Source: [[2026-06-18-email-thread-bergland]])
- Described internally as "our largest customer" by [[sandra-koller]]. (Source: [[2026-06-18-email-thread-bergland]])
- Planned its expansion for September 2026 around AlpPick 2.0. (Source: [[2026-06-18-email-thread-bergland]])

> [!warning] Contradiction – credit note
> [[thomas-rueegg]] (17 June 2026): "thank you for the commitment … Alpstein will compensate us for the outages. Please send me the credit note by the end of the month."
> [[jonas-weber]] (16 June 2026, to Rüegg): "On the downtime: I think we can do something about that. I will see what is possible."
> [[jonas-weber]] (18 June 2026, internal to Sandra Koller): "I did not commit to anything concrete."
> Whether a credit note was promised is not clear from the sources. Not resolved. (Source: [[2026-06-18-email-thread-bergland]])

- The Bergland case is cited as the lesson behind the rule that the service agent may **never** promise a credit note or compensation ([[agentic-ai-in-service]], 21 August 2026). (Source: [[2026-08-21-kickoff-notes-agentic-ai-pilot]])
